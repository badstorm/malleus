## What you may do without asking

1. **Refer to the project.** Say that your product, article, benchmark or talk is about Malleus, runs on it,
   integrates with it, or is compatible with it. "A Malleus backend for X" is fine.
2. **Fork it.** The license lets you modify and redistribute the code; describe the result as "a fork of Malleus",
   "based on Malleus" or "derived from Malleus". A fork is a good thing, it just needs its own name.
3. **Redistribute official builds unmodified** under the name — inside a distribution, a package, a container
   registry — stating the version and where it came from.
4. **Use the name for community activity** that is not a product: user groups, meetups, tutorials, chat channels.

## What needs our written permission

1. **Shipping a modified build under the name.** If you change the engine, the kernels, the defaults or the
   behaviour, the result gets its own name. Descriptive references ("a fork of Malleus") are fine.
2. **Using the name, or part of it, in the name of another product**, company, domain or image.
3. **Implying endorsement**: that a fork, a service or a product is official, sponsored or approved by the project
   owner, when it is not.
4. **Using the logo** as the mark of your product or service.

## Measured numbers published under the name

If you publish performance figures about this engine, state the **version** and what produced them (the model file,
the prompt set, the flags, the machine). It is not a condition of the license: it is what makes the number
reproducible, and a number that cannot be reproduced is worse than no number.

## Asking

For anything in the second list, write to the project owner. Requests that keep the meaning of the name intact —
"this is a fork, here is the source, here is the version" — are normally answered yes.
